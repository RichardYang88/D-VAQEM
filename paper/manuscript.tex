\abstract[ABSTRACT]{
Variational quantum sensors are calibrated on low-noise data but deployed on
hardware whose noise biases the very statistics the learned decoder inverts.  We
introduce \dvaqem{}, a variational quantum error mitigation scheme that acts on
the \emph{distribution} of measurement outcomes rather than on expectation
values: a small parametric map, trained only on calibration data at known
reference phases, pushes the noisy outcome distribution back toward the
noiseless one before a frozen, already certified decoder reads out the estimate.
The map acts on the collective population imbalance, so an $N$-qubit probe needs
$N+1$ numbers per phase instead of $2^N$ outcomes, and a readout trained on all
$2^N$ outcome probabilities is no more accurate in squared wrapped phase error.
A shot-aware
objective adds the exact
delta-method variance of the decoded estimate to the distribution-matching loss.
On an eight-qubit variational interferometer with depolarizing, dephasing,
amplitude-damping and readout noise at sixteen strengths, \dvaqem{} lowers the
squared wrapped phase error by $33.2$~dB at infinite shots (95\% paired
bootstrap CI $[27.8,38.0]$) and by $10.7$--$20.5$~dB at $256$--$4096$ shots,
beating zero-noise extrapolation in every setting and matching noise-aware
decoder retraining with two orders of magnitude fewer parameters.
}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Quantum sensors convert a physical quantity into a phase and read it out from
measurement statistics \cite{degen2017quantum,pezze2018quantum}.  Two
developments now meet in this field.  On the one hand, hybrid
quantum--classical estimators learn the inversion from statistics to phase
end-to-end, which yields globally unambiguous estimation over the full $2\pi$
range \cite{fiderer2021neural,gebhart2023learning,yang2025vqcnni} and reaches
metrological performance that linear estimators miss
\cite{marciniak2022optimal,kaubruegger2021quantum}.  On the other hand, the
devices that would run them are noisy: every gate deposits entropy into the
probe and every readout flips outcomes, and both effects deform the measured
distribution in a way that a decoder calibrated on clean data misreads as a
different phase.  For a learned estimator this is particularly damaging,
because the decoder is a fixed nonlinear inverse map: a systematic deformation
of its input is not averaged away by more shots, it is a bias.

Error mitigation for variational algorithms is a mature toolbox
\cite{cai2023quantum,bharti2022noisy}.  Zero-noise extrapolation (ZNE)
amplifies the noise by unitary folding and extrapolates observables to the
zero-noise limit \cite{li2017efficient,temme2017error,endo2018practical,
giurgica2020digital}; probabilistic error cancellation inverts a known noise
model at the price of a sampling overhead \cite{temme2017error,endo2018practical};
readout errors are undone by inverting a calibrated confusion matrix
\cite{maciejewski2020mitigation,bravyi2021mitigating}; and learning-based
schemes, collectively variational quantum error mitigation (VAQEM), train a
parametric mitigation layer on calibration data
\cite{strikis2021learning,czarnik2021qubit,czarnik2021error,lowe2021unified}.
Three limitations remain when these tools are applied to a \emph{learned}
metrological estimator.  (i) They mitigate \emph{expectation values}, whereas a
neural decoder consumes the whole outcome distribution, so the object that must
be repaired is a probability vector, not a scalar.  (ii) They live in the
$2^N$-dimensional outcome space, so the mitigation layer and the calibration
data grow exponentially with the probe size.  (iii) They are shot-agnostic: the
same mitigated object is used whether the device is sampled $10^2$ or $10^5$
times, although the optimal bias--variance trade-off of a mitigated estimator
depends strongly on the shot budget.

We address all three.  Our starting point is structural.  For the collective
probe considered here the measurement statistics are aggregated onto the
population imbalance $m(x)=(\#0(x)-\#1(x))/2\in\{-N/2,\dots,N/2\}$, an $(N+1)$-outcome
statistic, and the whole method lives in that reduced space.  Whether working
there is affordable is a question we answer by measurement rather than by
assumption
(Section~\ref{subsec:sufficiency}): for the same frozen probe we train a second
readout that consumes all $2^N$ outcome probabilities, under a protocol matched
to the certified decoder in every other respect---same training phases, same
circular loss, same optimizer, same hidden width, same early stopping---and
compare the two in the squared wrapped phase error (SWPE), the single
performance scale used throughout this paper.  The reduced readout is not the
weaker one.  Against the certified sector decoder the full-outcome readout is
ahead in only $9$ of the $28$ noisy conditions at infinite shots, and the
reduced readout is better by $1.03$~dB on average there, by up to $6.3$~dB;
under the matched protocol the two representations differ by $0.26$~dB in median
($0.70$~dB in mean) over all $32$ system-size $\times$ noise pairs, with a
single small-system exception of $6.8$~dB.  What the reduction buys, on the
other hand, is exponential: $N+1$ calibration numbers per phase instead of
$2^N$, and $(N+1)^2$ map parameters instead of the $4^N$ that a full-outcome
correction would need---$81$ instead of $65\,536$ at $N=8$.

On this space we define \dvaqem{} (Section~\ref{sec:method}): a parametric map
$M_{\bm\omega}$ from the noisy sector distribution to a corrected one, trained on
calibration data only---pairs of noisy and noiseless distributions at known
reference phases, the standard VAQEM resource assumption
\cite{strikis2021learning}---with four objectives (least squares,
cross-entropy, a score-matching regularizer, and a
shot-aware objective) over two map families (an $(N+1)^2$-parameter linear map
and a $1673$-parameter multilayer perceptron).  The shot-aware objective is our
second technical contribution: using the delta method
\cite{vaart1998asymptotic} we derive the exact leading-order variance of the
decoded phase under multinomial sampling and add it to the bias term, so the
map is fitted at the shot budget at which it will be used; the same formula
validates our finite-shot error bars analytically, without Monte Carlo.

The numerical study (Section~\ref{sec:results}) covers sixteen noise settings on
an eight-qubit variational interferometer, a shot-budget sweep from $64$ to
$8192$ shots, a scaling study to ten qubits, a calibration-budget study, and
three-seed and cold-start robustness checks, all produced by an exact
Kraus-operator simulator cross-validated against PennyLane
(Appendix~\ref{app:validation}).  \dvaqem{} reduces the mean squared wrapped
phase error by $33.2$ dB at infinite shots (95\% paired bootstrap CI
$[27.8,38.0]$) and closes $65$--$76\,\%$ of the
decibel distance to the noiseless estimator at finite shots; it beats every
ZNE variant in all sixteen settings, is exactly the remedy for readout noise
where ZNE is provably blind, and matches noise-aware retraining of the decoder
at finite shots while using $81$--$1673$ instead of $9666$ trainable parameters
and leaving the certified decoder untouched.  Each of these comparisons is
reported with a paired bootstrap interval and an exact test over the sixteen
noise settings rather than as a bare point estimate
(Section~\ref{subsec:noise}, Tables~\ref{tab:ci}--\ref{tab:ci_claims}), so the
reader can check not only the size of an effect but whether it is resolvable at
this sample size.  We also quantify what is lost by
not knowing the noise model: a genie estimator with the exact noise model and
$361$ exact simulations remains $46$ dB better at infinite shots (CI
$[40,53]$ dB), which is the
price \dvaqem{} pays for being model-free.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Probe, decoder and the calibration--inference protocol}
\label{subsec:protocol}

We use the variational quantum--classical neural-network interferometer
(VQ-CNNI) of Ref.~\cite{yang2025vqcnni} as the working estimator; the
construction below applies to any probe whose readout is aggregated onto a
collective statistic.  An unknown phase $\phi\in[-\pi,\pi)$ is imprinted on an
$N$-qubit probe by the Ramsey-type circuit of that reference,
\begin{equation}
  U_R(\phi;\bm\theta,\bm\vartheta)
  = R_x\!\Big(\frac{\pi}{2}\Big)\,U_{\rm De}(\bm\vartheta)\,R_z(\phi)\,
    U_{\rm En}(\bm\theta)\,R_y\!\Big(\frac{\pi}{2}\Big),
  \label{eq:probe}
\end{equation}
where $U_{\rm En}(\bm\theta)$ and $U_{\rm De}(\bm\vartheta)$ are one encoding and
one decoding layer of collective rotations and one-axis twisting terms, with the
same symbol conventions as Ref.~\cite{yang2025vqcnni}.  Their trainable parameter
vectors $\bm\theta$ (encoding layer) and $\bm\vartheta$ (decoding layer) are two
distinct objects, and both are distinct from the unknown phase $\phi$ and from
the parameters $\bm\omega$ of the mitigation map introduced in
Section~\ref{sec:method}; no symbol is reused for two of them anywhere in this
paper.  Measuring in the computational basis gives the outcome distribution
$p(x|\phi)=\big|\langle x|U_R(\phi;\bm\theta,\bm\vartheta)
|\downarrow\rangle^{\otimes N}\big|^2$ over bit strings $x\in\{0,1\}^N$, which we
aggregate onto the population imbalance between the two spin states,
written $m(x)=(\#0(x)-\#1(x))/2\in\{-N/2,-N/2+1,\dots,N/2\}$ as in
Ref.~\cite{yang2025vqcnni} (half-integer valued when $N$ is odd),
\begin{equation}
  p_{\bm\theta,\bm\vartheta}(m|\phi)
  = \sum_{x:\,m(x)=m} p(x|\phi)
  \equiv \big(A\,p(\cdot|\phi)\big)_m ,
  \label{eq:aggregate}
\end{equation}
so that the readout sees the $(N+1)$-component probability vector
$\pvec(m|\phi)=\big(p(-N/2|\phi),\dots,p(N/2|\phi)\big)$, a point of the
probability simplex $\Delta^N$, instead of the $2^N$
outcome probabilities; where a subscript is typographically convenient we write
$\pvec_m(\phi)\equiv\pvec(m|\phi)$.  The figures and the implementation label the
same outcomes by the integer imbalance $2m(x)=\#0(x)-\#1(x)\in\{-N,-N+2,\dots,N\}$; a
sector is the set of outcomes with a given $|m|$, of which there are
$\lfloor N/2\rfloor+1$.  The estimator is a frozen multilayer
perceptron $\phihat=D_{\mathbf w}\big(\pvec(m|\phi)\big)$ that maps this vector
to a phase through a circular output: the architecture is
$(N{+}1)\to128\to64\to2$ with softsign hidden layers, its two outputs are
$\ell_2$-normalized to a point $(v_0,v_1)$ on the unit circle and read as
$\phihat=\mathrm{atan2}(v_0,v_1)\in[-\pi,\pi)$ (the estimate written
$\tilde\phi$ in Ref.~\cite{yang2025vqcnni}); at $N=8$ this \emph{certified
decoder} has $9666$ parameters.  $D_{\mathbf w}$ and the circuit are trained
jointly on noiseless data with the circular loss $2[1-\cos(\phihat-\phi)]$ over
$100$ reference phases, exactly as in Ref.~\cite{yang2025vqcnni}; in this work
the trained pair is \emph{frozen} and treated as a certified sensor.  Every
other network appearing below is a different and much smaller object: the
mitigation maps of Section~\ref{sec:method} have $81$--$1673$ parameters, act on
the distribution rather than on the phase, and never modify $D_{\mathbf w}$.

Deployment follows the calibration--inference protocol of
Ref.~\cite{yang2025vqcnni}: reference phases are only ever used during
calibration, and inference on an unknown phase is a single forward pass.  We
keep this protocol and add one calibration resource, the standard one of
VAQEM \cite{strikis2021learning}: for $n_{\rm cal}$ known phases $\{\phi_i\}$
we collect the \emph{noisy} sector distributions $\yvec(\phi_i)$ from the
device and the corresponding \emph{noiseless} targets $\tvec(\phi_i)$ from a
classical model of the same circuit.  No knowledge of the noise channel, no
tomography and no folded circuits are required.

\subsection{Noise model, estimators and metric}
\label{subsec:noise}

Noise is a Markovian single-qubit channel $\Lambda$ (depolarizing, dephasing or
amplitude damping of strength $p$) applied after every gate, plus a symmetric
readout bit-flip of probability $q$; the full circuit is simulated exactly as a
Kraus density-matrix evolution (Appendix~\ref{app:validation}).  We write the
noisy sector family as $\yvec(\phi)=\mathcal N_\phi[\pvec_m(\phi)]$.  A
mitigation map $M_{\bm\omega}:\mathbb R^{N+1}\to\mathbb R^{N+1}$ produces a
corrected distribution
$\tilde\pvec = \mathrm{renorm}(\max(M_{\bm\omega}(\yvec),0))$ and the
mitigated estimate $\phihat = D_{\mathbf w}(\tilde\pvec)$.

The single performance scale of this paper is the squared wrapped phase error
(SWPE), $\mathrm{SWPE}=[\mathrm{arg}\,e^{i(\phihat-\phi)}]^2\in[0,\pi^2]$, the
circular analogue of a squared error and the natural score for an estimator that
has to be globally unambiguous over $[-\pi,\pi)$ \cite{yang2025vqcnni}.  It is
averaged over the held-out test grid within one Monte-Carlo trial, converted to
decibels, $\mathrm{SWPE}_{\rm dB}=10\log_{10}\mathrm{SWPE}$, and reported as the
mean of those per-trial decibel values: a lower number is always better, a
difference in decibels is a ratio of errors, and the convention is exactly the
one the paired bootstrap below recomputes inside every replicate.  Test phases
are half-offset from the calibration grid and lie in $[-\pi,\pi)$ so that no
physical phase is counted twice.  Finite-shot evaluation draws $S$ samples per
phase from the exact noisy distribution and repeats the whole experiment over
$41$ independent Monte-Carlo trials.  The random seed is offset by the shot
budget, so that at a given $S$ every method is evaluated on \emph{the same}
multinomial draws; all method-to-method comparisons at fixed $S$ are therefore
paired, which is what the bootstrap confidence intervals of
Section~\ref{sec:results} exploit.

\subsubsection{Statistical protocol}
Uncertainty is quantified by a paired percentile bootstrap with $B=20\,000$
replicates, and the resampling unit is matched to the way each number is
produced.  At a finite shot budget the $41$ Monte-Carlo trials are resampled and
the decibel average is recomputed inside every replicate, which reproduces
exactly the $\mathrm{SWPE}_{\rm dB}$ convention used throughout---the mean of
per-trial decibels, not the decibel of the mean---so each interval is centered on
the number we quote.  At infinite shots the evaluation is deterministic and the
$21$ held-out test phases are resampled instead.  The pairing above is preserved
inside every replicate, so an interval for a difference is an interval for a
\emph{paired} difference and not for two independent means.  Aggregates over the
sixteen noise settings resample the settings, since those rather than the test
phases are the population such a mean generalizes over.  Alongside each interval
we report the exact sign test and the exact Wilcoxon signed-rank test over the
sixteen settings; the Wilcoxon null distribution is enumerated by a subset-sum
recursion over the tied ranks rather than replaced by a normal approximation.
Before any interval is formed, a self-check verifies that every stored per-phase
and per-trial array reproduces the published $\mathrm{SWPE}$ and
$\mathrm{SWPE}_{\rm dB}$ to machine precision.  The complete tables are
Tables~\ref{tab:ci}--\ref{tab:ci_claims}.  The bootstrap and the two exact tests
are implemented from scratch rather than taken from a statistics library; they
are cross-validated against the corresponding library routines on tie-free data
and against brute-force enumeration of all $2^{16}$ sign patterns on tied data,
and the interval itself is checked for nominal coverage on synthetic data with a
known gain.

\subsection{What the sector reduction costs, and what it saves}
\label{subsec:sufficiency}
\label{sec:setup}

The reduction \eqref{eq:aggregate} is what makes distribution-level mitigation
affordable; whether it is also accurate is a question we settle by measurement
rather than by an information-theoretic argument.  There is a structural reason
to expect it to be cheap, and it is worth stating because it claims no bound: in
the circuit \eqref{eq:probe} the phase enters only through $R_z(\phi)$, which is
diagonal in the computational basis and therefore multiplies the amplitude of a
bit string $x$ by a phase that depends on $x$ only through its imbalance $m$.
The conditional distribution of the bit string \emph{given} its imbalance is thus
independent of $\phi$ (Appendix~\ref{app:sector}), and a readout bit-flip
channel, acting after the measurement, commutes with the aggregation.  Whatever
the phase does to the outcome distribution is therefore visible in the $N+1$
sector probabilities.

We test what that structure is worth on the error scale of the paper.  For each
system size $N\in\{4,6,8,10\}$ we train a second readout that consumes the full
outcome distribution $p(x|\phi)\in\mathbb R^{2^N}$ instead of the sector vector
$\pvec(m|\phi)\in\mathbb R^{N+1}$, holding everything else fixed: the same frozen
probe circuit of the checkpoint, the same $100$ noiseless training phases, the
same circular loss, the same Adam step size, the same hidden width $128$, the
same softsign activation and $\ell_2$-normalized circular output, and the same
early-stopping rule on the same $50$ half-offset phases.  The representation is
the only difference between the two arms, so a difference in SWPE is the price of
the reduction.  Both arms are evaluated on the paper's held-out test grid, on the
noiseless distribution and on seven noisy conditions, at infinite shots and at
$S=256,1024,4096$ with identical multinomial draws for the two arms; the
certified decoder of the checkpoint is carried along as a third arm, because it,
and not a freshly trained network, is what \dvaqem{} deploys.

Three things come out of it (Figure~\ref{fig:sufficiency},
Table~\ref{tab:e1}).  First, the reduction is not what limits the estimator: at
infinite shots the full-outcome readout beats the certified sector decoder in
only $9$ of the $28$ noisy conditions, and over those conditions the reduced
readout is better by $1.03$~dB on average and by up to $6.3$~dB; at $S=1024$ the
certified sector decoder is ahead in $20$ of the $32$ system-size $\times$ noise
pairs.  Second, when the two arms are matched in training protocol as well as in
probe---which removes the certified decoder's own fitting error from the
comparison---the two representations land within a fraction of a decibel of each
other: over all $32$ pairs at infinite shots the median gap is $0.26$~dB and the
mean is $0.70$~dB in favor of the full readout, while the reduced readout is
ahead by up to $2.0$~dB.  The single large exception is $6.8$~dB at $N=6$ under
depolarizing noise at $p=0.005$, a small-system setting in which the freshly
trained full readout happens to generalize better; we report it rather than
average it away.  Third, that price is trivial next to what the reduction buys:
$N+1$ calibration numbers per phase instead of $2^N$ ($9$ instead of $256$ at
$N=8$), a mitigation map of $(N+1)^2$ parameters instead of the $4^N$ that a
full-outcome correction would need ($81$ instead of $65\,536$), and a readout
network of $9666$ parameters instead of $41\,282$.  For scale, the mitigation
itself recovers $33.2$~dB (Section~\ref{subsec:sweep}), one to two orders of
magnitude more than the whole representation question is worth.

The noiseless rows carry one caution.  There every arm sits at its clean-data
fitting floor---a SWPE below $-55$~dB at all four system sizes---so the decibel
differences of that row measure how far each network was optimized rather than
what its input representation carries, and we do not read them as an accuracy
statement.  Everything we do next happens in the $(N+1)$-dimensional sector
space.

\subsection{Distribution-level mitigation maps}
\label{sec:method}

\dvaqem{} inserts a parametric, phase-independent map between the noisy
device and the frozen certified decoder: the device supplies the sector
distribution $\yvec$, the map returns a corrected distribution
$\tilde\pvec$, and the estimator itself is never modified
(Figure~\ref{fig:concept}a).  Everything below happens in the
$(N+1)$-dimensional sector space established in Section~\ref{sec:setup}.

\begin{figure*}[!t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig1_concept.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Distribution-level error mitigation.}
\textbf{a}~Data flow.  The probe circuit imprints the unknown phase; the
outcome statistics are aggregated onto the population imbalance
$2m(x)=\#0(x)-\#1(x)$; the noisy device deforms the sector distribution
$\yvec=\mathcal N_\phi[\pvec_m]$; a parametric map $M_{\bm\omega}$ corrects it
before the frozen, certified decoder reads out $\phihat$.  The map is fitted
once on calibration data---noisy distributions at known reference phases
together with noiseless targets from a classical model of the same
circuit---and never sees the test phases or the noise model.
\textbf{b}~The three sector distributions at $\phi=0.6$ rad for depolarizing
$p=0.01$, $N=8$: noiseless target, noisy input, and the deployed linear
shot-aware map output (the exact parameters stored with the results).
\textbf{c}~What those three distributions are worth to the estimator: the SWPE
of the same certified decoder at each of the $21$ held-out phases, on noiseless
data (mean $-65.3$~dB), on the noisy device (mean $1.7$~dB, and up to $9.4$~dB
at the worst phase, against the $5.2$~dB of a uniform guess over the phase
range), and after the deployed map (mean $-40.6$~dB).}
\label{fig:concept}
\end{figure*}

We consider two parametric families of maps on the sector space.  The
\emph{linear} map $\tilde\pvec = T\yvec$ with
$T\in\mathbb R^{(N+1)\times(N+1)}$ has $(N+1)^2=81$ parameters at $N=8$; it
contains the exact inverse of any flip-equivalent channel (readout bit-flip,
and depolarizing noise to leading order) and is convex in $T$ for the
least-squares objective.  The \emph{mitigation MLP}
$\tilde\pvec = W_3\,\sigma(W_2\,\sigma(W_1\yvec+b_1)+b_2)+b_3$ with hidden
widths $(32,32)$ has $1673$ parameters and can represent nonlinear corrections
such as the renormalization of a clipped quasi-probability.  This network is a
different object from the certified decoder of Section~\ref{subsec:protocol},
and the two are never used interchangeably: the mitigation MLP maps a
distribution to a distribution with hidden widths $(32,32)$ and $1673$
parameters, is refitted from calibration data for each noise condition, and is
discarded after use, whereas the decoder maps a distribution to a phase with
hidden widths $(128,64)$ and $9666$ parameters at $N=8$, is trained once on
noiseless data together with the probe circuit, and stays frozen throughout.
Both maps are followed
by clipping at zero and renormalization, so the decoder always receives a
probability vector; the clip is what makes the mitigated object a
quasi-probability in intermediate steps, exactly as in probabilistic error
cancellation \cite{temme2017error,endo2018practical}, except that here the
negative part is learned rather than derived from a noise model.

\subsection{Training objectives}
\label{subsec:objectives}

All objectives compare the mitigated family $\tilde\pvec(\phi_i)$ with the
noiseless target $\tvec(\phi_i)$ on the calibration grid; none of them uses the
test phases or any property of the noise channel.

\subsubsection{Least squares and cross-entropy}
$\mathcal L_{\ell_2}=\frac1{n_{\rm cal}}\sum_i\lVert
M_{\bm\omega}(\yvec_i)-\tvec_i\rVert^2$ and
$\mathcal L_{\rm CE}=-\frac1{n_{\rm cal}}\sum_i\sum_k t_{ik}\log
\tilde p_{ik}$.  The latter is the multinomial maximum-likelihood matching of
the clean family \cite{casella2002statistical} and is the distribution-level
analogue of the scalar VAQEM cost \cite{strikis2021learning}; it is sensitive to
the small-probability tails that carry the phase response.

\subsubsection{Score-matching regularizer}
Matching two distributions point by point does not by itself guarantee that the
\emph{response} of the family to the phase is preserved, and it is that response
the decoder inverts.  We therefore also fit the score-matching distance of
Ref.~\cite{hyvarinen2005estimation} as a regularizer,
\begin{equation}
  D_{\rm SM}(\tvec,\tilde\pvec)=\sum_k t_k\Big(\partial_\phi\log \tilde p_k
  - \partial_\phi\log t_k\Big)^2 ,
  \label{eq:df}
\end{equation}
which penalizes a mismatch of the logarithmic phase-derivatives (the scores) of
the two families, weighted by the target so that the low-probability sectors,
where the relative response is largest, are the ones that dominate; the
$\phi$-derivatives are central differences on the calibration grid.  The
objective is $\mathcal L_{\rm SM}=\mathcal L_{\rm CE}+\lambda D_{\rm SM}$ of
\eqref{eq:df} with $\lambda=1$, a value fixed a priori and never tuned: it is the only
hyperparameter of the four objectives that the protocol does not dictate, and we
state it explicitly for that reason.  We also state its outcome, because it is
negative.  At the same map family the regularized fit is never better than the
plain cross-entropy fit---identical to it in $10$ of the $16$ settings and behind
by up to $8.1$~dB in the rest, $2.0$~dB behind on average---and the held-out
criterion of Section~\ref{subsec:selection} deploys it in $1$ of the $16$
settings, where it ties with the unregularized fit.  The variant stays in the
suite because a selection rule that may pick it must also be allowed to reject
it, and because the comparison is precisely the ablation that tells a reader what
the term is worth: nothing measurable here.  What carries the results is the
shot-aware objective below, which the same criterion selects in $15$ of the $16$
settings ($11$ with the linear map, $4$ with the mitigation MLP).

\subsubsection{Shot-aware objective}
Standard VAQEM objectives are bias-only: they are the infinite-shot limit.  At
a finite shot budget $S$ the empirical distribution $\hat\yvec$ is multinomial
around $\yvec$, and the decoded estimate $\phihat=f(\hat\yvec)$, with
$f=D_{\mathbf w}\circ M_{\bm\omega}\circ\mathrm{clip}$, inherits a variance that
the map can control.  Scoring a predictor by the sum of a squared bias and a
variance term is the classical bias--variance decomposition
\cite{geman1992neural}, and fitting a model together with the noise it will be
evaluated under is standard in heteroscedastic regression
\cite{nix1994estimating,kendall2017uncertainties}; what makes the variance term
exact rather than learned here is that the sampling noise of a smooth readout of
a multinomial is available in closed form through the delta method
\cite{vaart1998asymptotic}.  For $f$ differentiable at $\yvec$ with gradient
$g=\nabla f(\yvec)$ and $\hat\yvec\sim\mathrm{Multinomial}(S,\yvec)/S$,
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{Var}[\phihat]=\frac1S\,g^{\mathsf T}\big(\mathrm{diag}\,\yvec-
  \yvec\yvec^{\mathsf T}\big)g + o(S^{-1}) ,
  \label{eq:var}
\end{equation}
and for a ZNE estimator $\phihat=f(\sum_f c_f \hat\yvec_f)$ with independently
sampled folds, $\mathrm{Var}[\phihat]=\frac1S\sum_f c_f^2\, g_f^{\mathsf T}
\Sigma_f g_f$, so the extrapolation coefficients amplify shot noise by
$\sum_f c_f^2\ge 1$; both statements are proved as Proposition~\ref{prop:delta}
in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.  The gradient $g$ is obtained by reverse-mode
differentiation through the frozen decoder and the map, so the whole expression
is analytic and cheap.  The shot-aware objective is then the predicted squared
wrapped phase error at the operating budget,
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal L_{S}(\bm\omega)=\frac1{n_{\rm cal}}\sum_i
  \Big\{2\big[1-\cos(\phihat_i-\phi_i)\big]
  + \mathrm{Var}_{S}[\phihat_i]\Big\},
  \label{eq:lse}
\end{equation}
whose first term is the circular loss the decoder itself was certified with and
whose second is \eqref{eq:var}, so the map is fitted on the scale on which it is
scored.  It reduces to the bias-only VAQEM cost as $S\to\infty$ and automatically
shrinks the map toward the low-variance regime at small $S$.  Equation
\eqref{eq:var} also exposes a structural weakness of ZNE that our numerics
confirm: Richardson extrapolation on folds $(1,3,5)$ has $\sum_f c_f^2=5.22$
and the quadratic fit on $(1,3,5,7,9)$ has $2.12$, whereas a contractive
learned map carries no such amplification.  Appendix~\ref{app:proofs} derives
both variance formulas and, in \S\ref{app:zne-blind}, the exact readout
blindness of unitary folding that makes \dvaqem{} the remedy for panel
\textbf{d} of Figure~\ref{fig:sweep}.

\subsection{Fitting, selection and what is held out}
\label{subsec:selection}

Each objective is fitted with Adam for $2000$ iterations on the $n_{\rm cal}$
calibration pairs; the least-squares fit starts from the identity map, the
cross-entropy fit is warm-started from it, the score-matching fit from the
cross-entropy fit, and the shot-aware fit \eqref{eq:lse} is attempted from a cold
start and from both infinite-shot fits.  Of the $n_{\rm cal}=21$ calibration
phases, $5$
are held out: the variant whose \emph{held-out} wrapped SWPE at the operating
shot count is smallest is the one deployed.  The test grid is never used for
selection; we report the agreement between the holdout choice and the
test-set-optimal choice only as a diagnostic ($7/16$ settings), and the
resulting selection is stable across random seeds ($16/16$ settings,
Section~\ref{subsec:robust}).  The whole procedure fits all seven variants in
$21.9\pm0.6$ s on six CPU cores at $N=8$, independently of $N$
(Table~\ref{tab:cost}).

\subsection{Baselines and what each of them assumes}
\label{subsec:baselines}

We compare against (i) the \emph{unmitigated} frozen decoder; (ii) three
distribution-level ZNE variants---Richardson and linear extrapolation on folds
$(1,3,5)$ and an over-determined quadratic fit on $(1,3,5,7,9)$, the strongest
extrapolation the data allow; (iii) the \emph{exact sector inverse}, the
analytic inverse of the flip-equivalent part of the channel---a \emph{genie}
form of readout-error mitigation that is told the exact effective flip rate,
available exactly only for readout noise, approximately for depolarizing noise,
and not at all for the two channel families that are not flip-equivalent
($8$ of the $16$ settings); (iv) the \emph{calibrated sector inverse}, which is
readout-error mitigation as a practitioner actually runs it: the same binomial
assignment matrix, but with its flip rate $\hat q$ estimated from the
calibration data alone, by fitting the exact sector kernel $K(\hat q)$ to the
calibration pairs on the same calibration phases the variational maps are
fitted on and with the held-out phases left unseen---so it consumes exactly the
resource budget \dvaqem{} gets, and no noise model; (v)
\emph{noise-aware decoder retraining}, which refits all $9666$ decoder
parameters on the same noisy calibration data---the strongest model-free
baseline, and the one that discards the certified decoder; and (vi) two
\emph{oracles} that know the exact noise model and evaluate it on a
$361$-point phase grid, reading out the phase by maximum likelihood (genie
estimators).  The noiseless decoder evaluated on clean data provides the floor
against which every method is measured, and the known-noise-model oracle the
ceiling that a model-free scheme cannot reach.

A seventh comparator, \emph{probabilistic error cancellation} (PEC), is evaluated
on its own grid rather than as a row of the headline table: it is the
quasi-probability form of (iv), sharing its assignment matrix but sampling the
inverse channel shot by shot instead of inverting it, so its purpose is to price
that route rather than to rank methods.  Its results, and the reason it is not a
row of Supplemental Tables~\ref{tab:sweep_inf}--\ref{tab:sweep_S1024}, are the
subject of Section~\ref{subsec:pec}.

Baselines (iii) and (iv) share one inversion machinery and differ only in where
the assignment matrix comes from, which is what makes the pair informative.  The
calibration in (iv) is not a handicap: on the four pure-readout settings
$\hat q$ reproduces the true flip rate to $3.8\times10^{-12}$, so the binomial
family contains the channel exactly there and the estimator recovers it.  On the
depolarizing settings the analytic rate of (iii) is only a flip-equivalent
\emph{surrogate} for the exact Kraus channel, and the calibrated rate---scoring
both on the same objective over the same calibration phases---fits the exact
channel better in $4$ of the $8$ settings where the analytic
rate exists at all.  Where (iv) loses, it therefore loses on the
\emph{model}---the assumption that the device acts as an independent per-qubit
bit flip---and not on the quality of its calibration.

\subsection{Headline comparison over four noise channels}
\label{subsec:sweep}

Figure~\ref{fig:sweep} and Table~\ref{tab:channels} collect the sixteen noise
settings; the complete method $\times$ setting matrices behind them are
Supplemental Tables~\ref{tab:sweep_inf} (infinite shots)
and~\ref{tab:sweep_S1024} ($S=1024$).  At infinite shots the unmitigated frozen
decoder degrades from its
noiseless floor of $-65.3$ dB to a mean of $-9.8$ dB; the holdout-selected
\dvaqem{} variant restores a mean of $-43.0$ dB, a mean reduction of $33.2$ dB
(95\% paired bootstrap CI $[27.8,38.0]$; median $34.7$ dB, range $4.2$--$53.0$
dB over settings).  The reduction is significant in every setting separately,
not merely on average: the exact sign and Wilcoxon signed-rank tests over the
sixteen settings both return $p=3.1\times10^{-5}$, the smallest value sixteen
paired observations can yield, and every per-setting paired interval excludes
zero (Table~\ref{tab:ci}).  It is
largest where the noise is strongest but a phase response still survives
($53.0$ dB at amplitude damping $p=0.02$) and smallest at dephasing $p=0.05$
($4.2$ dB), a setting in which the unmitigated estimator already sits at
$4.8$~dB: a uniform guess over the full phase range scores $5.2$~dB, so there is
essentially no phase response left to recover, and no method in our suite
recovers more.

Four comparisons isolate what the distribution-level formulation buys.  The
fourth---against readout-error mitigation---is the most diagnostic of them,
because that baseline is \emph{exact} in its own regime: it separates the value
of learning the map from the data from the value of merely not assuming a
bit-flip model.
First, \dvaqem{} beats the best of the three ZNE variants in all sixteen
settings, by $25.0$ dB on average (minimum $4.0$ dB).  The claim survives
per-setting testing rather than resting on an average: the paired interval
excludes zero in all sixteen cases, and the least favorable setting on its own
(dephasing $p=0.05$) still gives $4.0$ dB $[2.1,8.4]$, one-sided
$p=5.0\times10^{-5}$ (Table~\ref{tab:ci_zne}).  It also survives discarding the
a-posteriori choice of extrapolation variant.  Taking each ZNE variant instead
as a \emph{fixed} comparator, chosen before the data are seen, \dvaqem{} again
wins all sixteen settings with exact sign and Wilcoxon
$p=3.1\times10^{-5}$---by $25.4$ dB $[21.1,29.4]$ on average against
Richardson
extrapolation, $30.4$ dB $[25.1,35.4]$ against the linear fit and $28.1$ dB
$[23.0,32.8]$ against the quadratic fit---so no post-hoc selection is doing the
work.  The gap has two distinct
origins.  For readout noise it is qualitative: unitary folding leaves a
readout channel invariant, so every ZNE variant returns \emph{exactly} the
unmitigated estimate (Table~\ref{tab:channels}, readout columns), whereas the
learned map inverts the flip kernel and approaches the noiseless floor---to
$0.06$ dB at the weakest readout rate ($-65.2$ dB against a floor of $-65.3$ dB
at $q=0.01$), but with a residual that grows to $15.2$ dB at $q=0.05$, where
the calibrated inverse of baseline (iv) is exact instead.  For gate noise the gap is
quantitative and is explained by Proposition~\ref{prop:delta}: the
extrapolation coefficients amplify both the residual model error and, at
finite shots, the sampling noise.  Second, against noise-aware decoder
retraining---the strongest model-free baseline---\dvaqem{} is within $\sim1$ dB
at infinite shots (retraining wins $15/16$ settings there, as expected for a
baseline with $6$--$120\times$ more parameters and direct access to the
estimator objective) and is statistically indistinguishable at finite shots
(mean $-24.8$ dB versus $-24.5$ dB at $S=1024$).  That parity is a tested
statement rather than an impression left by two similar numbers: the paired mean
difference is $0.28$ dB $[-0.01,0.55]$ at $S=256$, $0.25$ dB
$[-0.17,0.62]$ at $S=1024$ and $0.04$ dB $[-0.69,0.62]$ at $S=4096$, and the
exact Wilcoxon test over the sixteen settings fails to reject equality at
$\alpha=0.05$ in all three cases ($p=0.105$, $0.193$, $0.375$).  \dvaqem{} is
nominally ahead in $10$--$11$ of the sixteen settings, but at this sample size
the difference is not resolvable---which is exactly the claim we make, and not a
stronger one.  It does so while using $81$--$1673$
instead of $9666$ trainable parameters, requiring no quantum--classical
re-optimization loop, and leaving the certified decoder untouched
(Table~\ref{tab:ci_claims}).  Third, the
known-noise-model oracle remains far ahead (mean $-89.1$ dB at infinite shots,
a $46.1$ dB gap to \dvaqem{}, 95\% CI $[40.3,52.7]$ dB): model-free mitigation
is not free, and the gap quantifies precisely what noise-model knowledge would
buy.  At finite shots the same gap collapses to $1.4$--$1.9$ dB, because there
the sampling variance common to both estimators dominates the residual model
error that separates them.  Knowing the noise model therefore pays off most
where shots are cheap, which is the opposite of the regime a deployed sensor
usually operates in.  Fourth, against readout-error mitigation the comparison
is the sharpest illustration of what the distribution-level formulation buys,
and it reverses between the infinite-shot limit and any realistic shot budget.
At infinite shots a correctly calibrated assignment matrix is a formidable
comparator and \dvaqem{} does \emph{not} beat it: the paired mean gain is
$6.7$ dB but the 95\% CI $[-1.4,15.0]$ contains zero, and both exact tests
fail to reject equality (sign $p=0.45$, Wilcoxon $p=0.30$; \dvaqem{} ahead in
only $10$ of $16$ settings).  That is the right answer, not a weakness of the
fit: on the four pure-readout settings the binomial family contains the channel
exactly, the inversion reaches the noiseless floor to within $1.2\times10^{-3}$
dB, and the $\ell_2$ map comes to $0.06$ dB of the floor at a $1\%$ flip rate
but $15.2$ dB of it at $5\%$.  At \emph{every} finite budget the ordering
flips, because inverting an ill-conditioned assignment matrix amplifies
sampling noise: \dvaqem{} gains $6.4$ dB $[3.3,9.8]$ at $S=256$, $7.9$ dB
$[4.2,12.0]$ at $S=1024$ and $9.5$ dB $[5.3,14.0]$ at $S=4096$, winning
$16/16$, $16/16$ and $15/16$ settings with exact sign $p\le5.2\times10^{-4}$.
The amplification is directly visible: in $4$ of the $64$ (setting, budget)
cells---all of them at finite shots, all of them dephasing or the strongest
depolarizing setting---the calibrated inverse leaves the estimate \emph{worse}
than not mitigating at all.  The same reversal holds against the genie inverse
that is told the exact flip rate, which is undefined in $8$ of the $16$
settings: \dvaqem{} is behind there on average at infinite shots ($-4.8$ dB
$[-11.4,2.1]$, not significant) and ahead at every finite budget ($5.3$ to
$6.1$ dB, exact Wilcoxon $p\le0.016$).  Readout-error mitigation is thus a
special case that is exactly optimal where its model holds and unlimited shots
are available; \dvaqem{} matches it there, and is the only one of the two that
survives a finite data set and a channel outside the flip family.

At finite shots the picture changes in \dvaqem{}'s favor relative to the
floor: the mean gain over the unmitigated estimator is $10.7$, $15.4$ and
$20.5$ dB at $S=256,1024,4096$, and the residual distance to the
\emph{noiseless} shot-noise floor is only $5.7$, $6.6$ and $6.4$ dB, i.e.\
\dvaqem{} closes $65$--$76\,\%$ of the decibel gap that noise opens between the
unmitigated estimator and the ideal device (Figure~\ref{fig:sweep}e,f).

\subsection{Finite shots: validating and exploiting the delta method}
\label{subsec:shots}

Figure~\ref{fig:shots} tests the closed-form variance \eqref{eq:var} on three
representative settings over $S=64\dots8192$.  The analytic prediction
$\mathrm{bias}^2+\mathrm{Var}_S$ tracks the Monte-Carlo SWPE over
$96$ (setting, method, $S$) points with a ratio in $[0.90,2.08]$, mean $1.03$,
median $1.00$ (Supplemental Table~\ref{tab:e3mc} for the Monte-Carlo values
and Table~\ref{tab:e3ratio} for every ratio); for the map-based estimators the ratio
stays in
$[0.90,1.18]$, and the only deviations beyond $1.2$ are ZNE at $S\le256$,
where the extrapolated observable is far from Gaussian.  The closed-form
variance is therefore trustworthy both as an error bar and as a training
signal.

Exploiting it is worth a lot.  Refitting the shot-aware map at the operating
budget improves on the shot-independent cross-entropy map by up to $8.6$ dB
(depolarizing), $12.8$ dB (amplitude damping) and $14.2$ dB (readout) at
$S=8192$, with the gain growing monotonically in $S$ as the bias term becomes
the limiting one (Figure~\ref{fig:shots}c); at $S=64$ the two coincide, as they
must, because both are then variance-dominated.  The mechanism is the one
\eqref{eq:var} predicts, and it is readable off the analytic terms themselves
rather than off a separate decomposition: at depolarizing $p=0.01$ the shot-aware
refit changes the variance term by at most $10\,\%$ at any budget, while its
squared bias falls from $3.4\times10^{-3}$ at $S=64$ to $9.7\times10^{-6}$ at
$S=8192$, a factor of $347$.  A cold-start ablation (Table~\ref{tab:ablation})
shows that the staged warm starts are a robustness device rather than a necessity
in this regime: refitting from scratch leaves the deployed SWPE $0.3$~dB better on
average and at most $1.4$~dB worse, and changes the selected variant in $1/16$
settings.

\subsection{Scaling to ten qubits}
\label{subsec:scaling}

The scaling study repeats the full method suite at $N=4,6,8,10$ with
depolarizing $p=0.01$ (Figure~\ref{fig:scaling}, Table~\ref{tab:scaling}), each
size using its own independently trained interferometer.  The unmitigated
estimator degrades with $N$ (from $-5.8$ dB at $N=4$ to $2.1$ dB at $N=10$ at
$S=1024$) because the same per-gate error rate acts on a larger probe; the
selected \dvaqem{} variant reduces the mean squared error by $30.1$, $44.2$,
$42.8$ and $34.7$ dB at infinite shots and by $19.2$, $23.6$, $25.9$ and
$18.3$ dB at $S=1024$, i.e.\ the mitigation does not decay with system size.
What changes with $N$ is the \emph{cost of the alternatives}: the calibration
data are $N+1=5\dots11$ numbers per phase and the linear map has
$(N+1)^2=25\dots121$ parameters, whereas a full-outcome map would need $4^N$
parameters ($1.05\times10^6$ at $N=10$) and process tomography of the probe
would need the same number of coefficients.  The measured wall-clock cost makes
the same point dynamically (Table~\ref{tab:cost}): fitting all seven
\dvaqem{} variants takes $21.6$--$22.8$ s at every $N$, the $21$-phase
calibration costs $0.01$--$48.5$ s of exact simulation, while the $361$-phase
oracle grid costs $0.1$ s at $N=4$ but $833$ s at $N=10$---and the underlying
exact dataset for the scaling study itself took $1982$ s at $N=10$.  At
$N=10$ the mitigated estimator ($-16.3$ dB at $S=1024$) still trails the
noiseless floor ($-25.9$ dB) by $\sim10$ dB, the largest residual in the study;
we attribute it to the combination of a device response that this noise level has
all but flattened---the unmitigated estimator sits at the level of a uniform guess
over the phase range---and only $21$ calibration phases for an
$11$-dimensional distribution, and regard closing it as the main quantitative
target for hardware experiments.

\subsection{Probabilistic error cancellation: the price of quasi-probability}
\label{subsec:pec}

Readout-error mitigation is usually deployed in its \emph{probabilistic} form:
sample the quasi-probability decomposition of the inverse channel shot by shot
rather than invert the assignment matrix.  The two are the same estimator in
expectation---our test suite verifies $M = K^{-\mathsf T}$ to machine precision,
and on the decoded phase they agree to $5.7\times10^{-3}$ dB across the $13$
settings whose overhead satisfies $\gamma\le20$ (largest $\gamma=18.1$ among
them)---so nothing but \emph{variance} can separate them, which makes PEC the
sharpest available probe of what a quasi-probability correction costs.  We
therefore ran it on its own grid: the $16$ settings of the headline sweep plus a
mixed depolarizing--readout setting and three stronger readout rates, $20$ in
total, with $25$ calibration phases, $41$ held-out test phases and $25$
Monte-Carlo trials per finite-shot cell.  All four comparators---calibrated PEC,
PEC handed the analytic rate, the deterministic inverse and no mitigation---are
recomputed on that one grid from a shared sampler seed, so every contrast below
is paired.  The grid deliberately differs from the headline sweep's ($21$
calibration and $21$ test phases, Supplemental
Tables~\ref{tab:sweep_inf}--\ref{tab:sweep_S1024}) and nothing is joined across
the two files, so the absolute decibel levels quoted here are not comparable to
that table; only the PEC-versus-inverse contrast, which is what this subsection
is about, is.

The overhead is the whole story.  The calibrated rate gives
$\gamma=(1-2\hat q)^{-N}$ between $1.18$ and $5.4\times10^{10}$ (median $8.51$),
and a PEC estimate carries $\gamma^2$ times the variance of the deterministic
one: up to $215$ dB of variance amplification at dephasing $p=0.05$, where
$\hat q=0.477$ approaches the $q=1/2$ singularity at which the channel ceases to
be invertible at all.  Calibration is not the limitation.  On the $7$
pure-readout settings, where the binomial family contains the channel exactly,
$\gamma$ is recovered to $7.3\times10^{-11}$ relative; on depolarizing noise,
where a single flip rate can only ever be a surrogate, the same fit is up to
$26\%$ off.  Hence PEC built on calibration data is physically indistinguishable
from PEC handed the analytic rate at every finite budget---the mean difference is
at most $0.05$ dB in magnitude and the exact Wilcoxon test fails to reject
equality at all three ($p\ge0.12$)---and the $0.9$ dB it concedes to the genie at
infinite shots $[-2.0,-0.2]$ is the only place where its model error is visible
at all.

The verdict is unambiguous, and it is the opposite of a success story.  Against
no mitigation PEC wins decisively at infinite shots ($31.1$ dB $[23.9,38.6]$,
ahead in $20/20$ settings, exact sign $p\le1.9\times10^{-6}$), yet at $S=256$ it
is statistically indistinguishable from doing nothing ($-0.3$ dB $[-0.9,0.4]$,
sign $p=0.50$) and is in fact \emph{worse} in $12$ of the $20$ settings; it needs
$S\ge1024$ before the gain resolves at all ($2.8$ dB $[1.4,4.2]$, exact
Wilcoxon $p=0.003$).  Against the deterministic calibrated inverse it loses
everywhere---ahead in $0/20$ settings at each of $S=256$, $1024$ and $4096$, by
$-4.6$ dB $[-6.3,-3.1]$, $-5.0$ dB $[-6.7,-3.5]$ and $-5.6$ dB $[-7.4,-3.9]$,
with exact sign and Wilcoxon $p\le1.9\times10^{-6}$ in all three---and even at
infinite shots the mean is $-0.9$ dB $[-2.09,-0.04]$, the exact Wilcoxon test
rejecting equality at $p=0.030$.  Canceling errors probabilistically is not
\emph{wrong}: it is unbiased, and it is the right instrument when shots are
free.  It is simply dominated, because it pays $\gamma^2$ for an expectation the
matrix inverse delivers exactly.  Supplemental Table~\ref{tab:pec} tabulates
every one of these contrasts per setting, ordered by the overhead $\gamma$ on
which they turn, so that the per-setting structure can be read off directly
rather than inferred from the aggregate means.

Two consequences matter for how such a baseline ought to be run.  First, where
the assignment matrix is ill-conditioned the two estimators do separate, and the
\emph{regularized} one is better: across the $7$ settings with $\gamma>20$ the
Tikhonov term inside the deterministic inverse buys up to $8.9$ dB (dephasing
$p=0.05$, $\gamma=5.4\times10^{10}$).  An unregularized exact inverse is not a
fair comparator there, and the gap is a property of the inversion rather than of
PEC.  Second, PEC does not escape model error either.  Mis-specifying the flip
rate by $0.02$ costs $19.1$ dB ($-39.2\to-20.1$ dB); the deterministic inverse
stays ahead at $6$ of the $7$ assumed rates, by up to $0.9$ dB; and the
worst-case residual \emph{sector} distance of the mismatched map is $0.25$.
That residual is a bias no amount of data removes---precisely the property a map
fitted to the device one actually has does not possess.  This is the sense in
which \dvaqem{} is model-free rather than model-agnostic: it is fitted to the
channel that is present, so it has neither the $\gamma^2$ variance penalty of a
quasi-probability correction nor the irreducible bias of a mis-specified one.


\subsection{Robustness: seeds, ablations and simulator validation}
\label{subsec:robust}

Repeating the entire headline sweep with three independent random seeds (map
initialization, holdout sampling and Monte-Carlo trials) selects the identical
variant in all $16$ settings every time; the per-setting spread of the deployed
SWPE is $0.00$ dB at infinite shots and at most $0.93$ dB (mean $0.46$ dB) at
$S=1024$, i.e.\ of the order of the Monte-Carlo error bar itself
(Table~\ref{tab:seeds}).  The cold-start ablation of
Section~\ref{subsec:shots} and the untuned score-matching weight ($\lambda=1$
throughout) leave the conclusions unchanged.  Finally, every distribution used
above comes from a simulator that reproduces PennyLane's \texttt{default.mixed}
to $\le3.5\times10^{-13}$ and the stored VQ-CNNI checkpoints to machine
precision, and for which the sector aggregation is verified to commute with the
readout noise kernel to $1.1\times10^{-16}$ (Appendix~\ref{app:validation}), so
the reported gains are properties of the method and not of our numerical
backend.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What the method assumes, and what it does not}
\dvaqem{} assumes a stationary noise channel over the calibration window, a
classical model of the noiseless circuit (or a low-noise device that provides
the targets), and a decoder that is frozen after certification.  It assumes
\emph{nothing} about the channel itself: no tomography, no noise model, no
folded circuits at inference, and no modification of the certified estimator.
The price of model-freedom is visible in our numbers as the $46$ dB gap to the
known-noise-model oracle at infinite shots; where a trustworthy noise model
exists the oracle remains the better tool, and \dvaqem{} is the tool for the
regime where no such model is available---which is the regime of most near-term
deployments.  Model knowledge is worth less than one might expect, though, as
soon as the correction has to be \emph{sampled}: probabilistic error
cancellation built on the same model is dominated by the deterministic inverse
in $20/20$ settings at every finite budget we tested
(Section~\ref{subsec:pec}), so the advantage of knowing the channel is largely
confined to the infinite-shot limit.

\subsection{Why distribution level, and why now}
The comparison of Section~\ref{subsec:sufficiency} is what turns
distribution-level mitigation from a heuristic into a quantified design choice:
a map on $N+1$ numbers gives up $0.26$~dB in median SWPE against a readout
trained on all $2^N$ outcome probabilities under a matched protocol, and nothing
at all against the certified decoder it is actually paired with---which it beats
in $19$ of the $28$ noisy conditions---while its parameter count and calibration
volume grow linearly instead of exponentially.  This is complementary to, and
compatible with, observable-level
VAQEM \cite{strikis2021learning,lowe2021unified}: whenever the readout of
interest is a linear observable, the two coincide; whenever the readout is a
learned nonlinear inverse---the situation in every neural quantum sensor
\cite{fiderer2021neural,gebhart2023learning,yang2025vqcnni}---only the
distribution-level object can be repaired without retraining the sensor.

\subsection{Limitations}
Our study is numerical: the noise is Markovian, gate-synchronous and stationary,
whereas hardware noise is correlated and drifting; extending the calibration
protocol to drifting channels (e.g.\ by interleaving recalibration phases) is
the natural next step.  The probe family is a single architecture (the
VQ-CNNI interferometer), although the structural argument of
Section~\ref{subsec:sufficiency} applies to any probe whose readout is
aggregated onto a collective statistic, and the four independently trained
checkpoints ($N=4,6,8,10$) already show that the conclusions are not tied to one
trained model.  The mitigated family is a clipped quasi-probability: the largest
clipped negative mass we observe is $0.10$, so what the decoder receives is a
genuine probability vector while the map that produces it is not a physical
channel, and a hardware implementation should expect the clip to be active.  And
at dephasing $p=0.05$ the channel has all but flattened the response of the
probe---the unmitigated estimator sits at the level of a uniform guess over the
phase range---so no mitigation, ours included, can recover a phase response that
the channel destroyed; the honest statement there is that the sensor should be
re-designed rather than post-processed.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We have shown that error mitigation for learned quantum sensors is naturally
formulated at the level of measurement distributions, and that for
collective-spin probes the reduced description this requires is not the weak
link: a readout trained on all $2^N$ outcome probabilities is no more accurate
than the certified decoder that reads the $N+1$ sector probabilities, while the
calibration volume and the number of map parameters grow linearly instead of
exponentially.  On this $(N+1)$-dimensional space a small parametric map, trained
on calibration data alone and selected by a held-out calibration score, reduces
the squared wrapped phase error of an eight-qubit variational interferometer by
$33.2$ dB at infinite shots (95\% paired bootstrap CI $[27.8,38.0]$) and closes
$65$--$76\,\%$ of the decibel gap to the noiseless
device at $256$--$4096$ shots, beating zero-noise extrapolation in all sixteen
noise settings (exact sign test $p=3.1\times10^{-5}$, every per-setting paired
interval excluding zero) and matching noise-aware decoder retraining at finite
shots (exact Wilcoxon $p\ge0.10$) with
two orders of magnitude fewer parameters.  A shot-aware objective built on the
exact delta-method variance makes the mitigation aware of the operating shot
budget and validates its own error bars analytically.  The approach scales to
ten qubits with eleven calibration numbers per phase and needs as few as five
calibration phases to buy $25$ dB.  Distribution-level mitigation is
therefore a practical, model-free route to deploying calibrated quantum sensors
on noisy hardware.

\section{Proofs and derivations}
\label{app:proofs}

\subsection{Why the sector reduction leaves the phase response intact}
\label{app:sector}
The probe \eqref{eq:probe} is built from gates that are all applied with the same
angle to every qubit---collective rotations $R_{x,y}$, Hadamards, the collective
phase imprint $R_z(\phi)^{\otimes N}$---together with $R_{zz}(\chi,i,j)$ terms of
identical strength on \emph{every} pair $(i,j)$, which realize the one-axis
twisting $e^{-i\chi J_z^2}$ up to single-qubit phases.  Each of them commutes
with any permutation of the qubits, and the input
$|\downarrow\rangle^{\otimes N}$ is permutation-invariant, so the state is
permutation-symmetric at every step of the circuit.  A permutation-symmetric
density matrix assigns the same probability to all bit strings with the same
number of excitations, hence
\begin{equation}
  p(x|\phi)=\frac{p(m|\phi)}{\binom{N}{(N+m)/2}}
  \qquad\text{for every }x\text{ with imbalance }m,
  \label{eq:uniform-sector}
\end{equation}
and the conditional distribution of the bit string given its sector is the
uniform one on that sector, $p(x|m,\phi)=1/\binom{N}{(N+m)/2}$: it does not
depend on $\phi$ at all.  Aggregating the $2^N$ outcomes onto the $N+1$ sectors
therefore discards a factor that carries no dependence on the parameter being
estimated, which is the structural reason the comparison of
Section~\ref{subsec:sufficiency} comes out as it does \cite{casella2002statistical}.
A readout bit-flip channel acts after the measurement, as a stochastic matrix on
the outcome probabilities, and commutes with the aggregation,
$A\,B_{\rm full}=B_m\,A$---an identity the simulator verifies to
$1.1\times10^{-16}$ (Appendix~\ref{app:validation})---so pure readout noise
changes nothing in the argument.  When a channel acts \emph{inside} the circuit
it breaks the permutation symmetry only through its own randomness, and the
conditional acquires a weak phase dependence; that is precisely why
Section~\ref{subsec:sufficiency} prices the reduction by measuring the SWPE of
the competing readouts instead of resting on \eqref{eq:uniform-sector}.

\subsection{Delta-method variance of a mitigated estimate}
\begin{proposition}[Delta-method variance of a mitigated estimate]
\label{prop:delta}
Let $f$ be differentiable at $\yvec$ with gradient $g=\nabla f(\yvec)$ and
$\hat\yvec\sim\mathrm{Multinomial}(S,\yvec)/S$.  Then
$\mathrm{Var}[\phihat]=\frac1S\,g^{\mathsf T}(\mathrm{diag}\,\yvec-
\yvec\yvec^{\mathsf T})g + o(S^{-1})$.  For a ZNE estimator
$\phihat=f(\sum_f c_f \hat\yvec_f)$ with independently sampled folds,
$\mathrm{Var}[\phihat]=\frac1S\sum_f c_f^2\, g_f^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_f g_f$, and
$\sum_f c_f^2\ge1$, with equality only for the trivial single-fold estimator.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $\hat\yvec=\yvec+S^{-1/2}\bm\xi$ with
$\mathbb E[\bm\xi]=0$ and
$\mathrm{Cov}[\bm\xi]=\Sigma=\mathrm{diag}\,\yvec-\yvec\yvec^{\mathsf T}$, the
multinomial covariance.  A first-order Taylor expansion of the differentiable
readout $f$ gives
$\phihat=f(\yvec)+S^{-1/2}g^{\mathsf T}\bm\xi+o_p(S^{-1/2})$, hence
$\mathrm{Var}[\phihat]=S^{-1}g^{\mathsf T}\Sigma g+o(S^{-1})$, which is the
first claim.  For ZNE the estimator is
$\phihat=f(\sum_f c_f\hat\yvec_f)$ with \emph{independent} folds, so the
chain rule gives the gradient $c_f g_f$ with respect to fold $f$ and
$\mathrm{Var}[\phihat]=S^{-1}\sum_f c_f^2 g_f^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_f g_f$.  Since
$\Sigma_f\succeq0$ and $\sum_f c_f=1$ for any extrapolation that is exact at
zero noise, Cauchy--Schwarz gives $\sum_f c_f^2\ge1$, with equality only for
the trivial single-fold estimator: extrapolation necessarily amplifies shot
noise.  The values quoted in the main text, $\sum_f c_f^2=5.22$ (Richardson on
$(1,3,5)$), $1.46$ (linear fit) and $2.12$ (quadratic fit on $(1,3,5,7,9)$),
are read off the fitted coefficients stored with the results.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Readout blindness of unitary folding}
\label{app:zne-blind}
Let the device noise be a readout channel $B$ acting on the sampled outcomes,
so that the measured family is $\yvec^{(f)}=B\,A\,p^{(f)}$ at fold $f$, where
$p^{(f)}$ is the ideal (noiseless) distribution of the folded circuit.  Unitary
folding satisfies $p^{(f)}=p^{(1)}$ for every odd $f$, because
$UU^\dagger=\mathbb 1$; hence $\yvec^{(f)}$ is independent of $f$ and any
extrapolation $\sum_f c_f \yvec^{(f)}$ with $\sum_f c_f=1$ returns exactly
$\yvec^{(1)}$, the unmitigated distribution.  This is why the ZNE columns for
readout noise in Table~\ref{tab:channels} coincide with the unmitigated row to
all printed digits.  The same argument shows that folding cannot mitigate any
channel that commutes with the folding operation, including measurement
assignment error, which is precisely the channel class that the learned
distribution-level map inverts.

\section{Simulator validation}
\label{app:validation}

All distributions in this work come from a NumPy/autograd Kraus
density-matrix simulator that applies an arbitrary single-qubit channel after
every gate and supports unitary folding.  It is validated by $53$ automated
checks, all of which pass:
(i) the noiseless statevector, real-representation and density-matrix paths
agree to $\le3\times10^{-15}$; (ii) trace preservation of every Kraus set and
normalization of every output distribution; (iii) fold invariance of the
noiseless circuit ($\le6\times10^{-15}$); (iv) exactness of the sector
reduction, $A K_{\rm full}=K_m A$ for the noisy sector kernel, to
$1.1\times10^{-16}$;
(v) agreement with PennyLane~0.42 \texttt{default.mixed} at $N=4$ for
depolarizing, dephasing, amplitude-damping and bit-flip channels and folds
$1,3,5$, with maximum deviation $3.5\times10^{-13}$ over outcome
probabilities, and agreement with the stored PennyLane-trained VQ-CNNI
checkpoints (median SWPE identical to all printed digits);
(vi) the flip-equivalent surrogate used only to accelerate the noiseless
calibration path deviates from the exact Kraus evolution by at most
$1.3\times10^{-2}$ in $p_m$ at the strongest depolarizing rate considered, and
is never used in any reported evaluation.  The full check log ships with the
code.
